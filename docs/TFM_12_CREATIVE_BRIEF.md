## The Mission in One Line

Teach people to escape Big Tech surveillance through free in-person events, hands-on Linux training, and a growth model that turns today's student into tomorrow's teacher.

---

## The Scriptural Anchor

**Galatians 5:1** — *"For freedom Christ has set us free; stand firm, therefore, and do not submit again to a yoke of slavery."*

This is not a tagline. It is the thesis. Digital sovereignty is a stewardship question, not a consumer question. The same impulse that drove the Reformation — putting the text in the hands of ordinary people — drives TFM. Big Tech is the yoke. Linux is the door.

---

## The Tagline

**"Giving freedom with a fishing pole."**

Teach, don't give. The fishing pole is the skill, the Roadmap, the community. TFM doesn't hand people freedom — it hands them the means to take it and then teach someone else.

---

## Who TFM Serves

**The entry point person:** Someone who has never thought about who owns their data. They show up because a friend told them there was a free event and they might get a laptop. They leave three hours later with a working machine they own outright and the first crack in a worldview they didn't know they had.

**The church community:** A congregation anywhere can host TFM. The venue, trust, and community infrastructure are already there. TFM supplies the hardware and the model. The rural church that can't afford a tech ministry gets one.

**The person who is almost there:** Someone who uses Linux at home, knows the terminal, has been doing this quietly. TFM gives them a name for what they already believe and a way to share it.

**The Vanguard:** The graduate who came back. Has the skills, earns the credential, runs their own events. The mission multiplies through them.

---

## The Three Brand Layers

TFM speaks differently depending on who is in the room. This is not inconsistency — it is strategic.

**Layer 1 — Faith / Church context:**
Lead with Galatians. Lead with stewardship. Lead with "we are not meant to be owned." The surveillance state is a spiritual problem. The answer is sovereignty. TFM is a ministry.

**Layer 2 — Secular / Privacy context:**
Lead with the data. Lead with "your phone reports on you to people you've never met." Don't mention scripture. Don't mention ministry. Lead with the event: free, three hours, working laptop. The mission is identical. The door is different.

**Layer 3 — TFM Culture / Member context:**
The full language. Vanguard. Roadmap. The Oath. Great Commission. Exodus. The hardware loop. These people know what they signed up for. Speak directly.

---

## The Growth Model

This is a self-replicating system, not a program with a budget line.

1. A donor gives obsolete hardware — what Big Tech deems end-of-life is someone's first computer.
2. TFM runs an Install Party — the hook is "free laptop for your time and attention."
3. The student installs Linux, replaces one cloud account, leaves with a machine they own.
4. They follow the Roadmap at their own pace.
5. At Level 4, they become a Vanguard — they run their own events.
6. Those events need hardware. The loop restarts.

No central staff needed to scale. A Vanguard in a city is TFM in that city.

## The Roadmap — Four Levels

**Level 1: The Exodus** *(color: dark forest green — `#1D4D2A` bg / `#A8D4B2` text)*
The first event. Three hours. Install Linux on a real machine. Replace one cloud account. Leave with a working laptop. Made possible by donated hardware.
*Callout: "Level 1 complete. You own the machine now. Nobody has root but you."*

**Level 2: The Catechism** *(color: deep gold-brown — `#5C4500` bg / `#F0C85A` text)*
The why behind the how. Navigate the terminal. Understand sudo. Install software without a mouse. The history and theology of digital sovereignty.
*Callout: "Level 2 complete. You understand the machine now. That changes how you see every other one."*

**Level 3: The Homestead** *(color: deep crimson — `#5C1A1A` bg / `#F0A8A8` text)*
You stop renting. Stand up a home server. Self-host your photos and files. Cut your last consumer cloud account. Donated hardware from the TFM pool supports members building their first home server.
*Callout: "Level 3 complete. You stopped renting. Your data lives where you put it, and nowhere else."*

**Level 4: The Great Commission** *(color: dark olive — `#2C2C1A` bg / `#C8C890` text)*
Run an Exodus event. Teach a full Catechism cycle. Take the Vanguard Oath.
*Callout: "Level 4 complete. Take the oath. Find your city. The next Exodus is yours to run."*

## The Vanguard

The Vanguard is the person who has been where the new student is — and came back.

**What is expected:**
- Level 3: The Homestead. Complete.
- Minimum 2 events per year. Show up and teach.
- Complete the TFM Field Manual at your own pace.
- Be reachable between events for people in your city.

**What they receive:**
- Their name on the Roadmap cards they hand out.
- A Vanguard credential card. Hard stock, gold accent.
- Early access to event intel and the Vanguard coordination channel.
- Hardware coordination support for the events they run. They do not source machines alone.
- First Vanguard in their city if no one else is there yet.

The Vanguard credential is earned, never bought. It is the physical proof of the Oath.

---

## The Vanguard Oath

*Taken at Level 4. Printed on the credential card.*

1. I will not sell what was given to me freely.
2. I will teach the next person what I was taught.
3. I will own the tools I use, and the data they produce.
4. I will not speak ill of those still inside the system; I was inside it once.
5. I will keep the Roadmap card on me. It is the proof and the promise.

## The Hardware Strategy

The primary donation ask is hardware, not cash.

**The conditional promise:** TFM cannot guarantee a free laptop without your generosity. Lead with what is impossible without the donation. Then state what becomes possible with it. Never state the laptop as a guarantee.

**Hardware priority order:**
Laptops → Workstation PCs → Mini PCs → Old gaming desktops → Tablets → Single Board Computers

**Hardware feeds all four levels:**
- Level 1: The donated machine someone walks out with
- Level 2: The terminal they learn on
- Level 3: The home server built from donated SBCs and mini PCs
- Level 4: The machines a Vanguard needs to run their own Exodus event

**Hardware contact:** techfreedomministries@proton.me

**Voice rule:** Stakes first, specs second. Lead with what is impossible. Close with what becomes possible.

## The Visual Language — Gilded Spine Theme

The primary visual metaphor is **a leather-bound Bible** — the physical object TFM most resembles. A document of lasting consequence. Handled repeatedly. Passed from person to person.

### Specific visual references
- Hand-tooled dark leather cover — the header surface
- Gilt page edges — gold gradient on the right edge of every card
- Crimson ribbon bookmark — fixed, hanging, pointed bottom
- Gold spine strip — full-height left edge of the viewport
- Hemp page texture — barely-visible woven grid on parchment background
- Colophon ornament — fading gold rules flanking ◆, Cinzel typeface

## The Voice

Short sentences. Declarative. The copy assumes the reader can handle direct.

**Examples:**
- *"Your phone reports on you to people you've never met."*
- *"We can't give free laptops without your generosity."*
- *"A Vanguard is the person who has been where the new student is. And came back."*
- *"Four levels. Each one earned, never bought."*
- *"Nobody has root but you."*
- *"What big tech deems obsolete becomes someone's first computer."*

**Rules:**
- Lead with stakes. Close with what becomes possible.
- Never soften the ask.
- Never bury the mission in positive framing.
- Never lead with scripture in secular contexts.
- Never lead with surveillance language in church contexts.

## What TFM Is Not

- A subscription service
- A product that sells you something after the free part
- An anti-tech grievance movement
- A church (works with churches — is not one)
- Funded by donations from any platform it teaches people to leave

## Where TFM Is Going

The church/rural model is the growth path. A congregation anywhere can host TFM events if TFM can supply hardware and a Vanguard. The venue, community, and faith context are already there.

The goal is not a central organization that runs everything. The goal is enough Vanguards in enough cities that the mission runs without anyone at the top having to push it.

That is what the Great Commission means in this context.

## Design Prompts — Use These When Generating Assets

For **print materials** (event flyers, Roadmap cards, Vanguard credential):
> Design in the visual language of a historical document — think field manual, press layout, theological text. Dark leather, parchment, gilt gold. EB Garamond. Cinzel for titles. No photography. No modern UI patterns. The aesthetic is "document of lasting consequence."

For **event photography direction**:
> Real rooms. Real people. Hands on keyboards. No posed smiles. No corporate staging. The energy is a workshop, not a conference.

For **social media assets**:
> Parchment background. Gold rule separators. Short declarative copy in EB Garamond. The ◆ diamond as a recurring mark. Never use a tech-brand color palette.

For **hardware donation appeals**:
> Lead with what is impossible without the donation. Show the before: an empty table at an Install Party, no laptops. Show the after: someone leaves with a machine they own. Never lead with specs. Stakes first, specs second.
