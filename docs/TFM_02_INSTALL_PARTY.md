# TFM_02 — THE INSTALL PARTY
*The entry event. The first sacrament. The spark.*

---

## What It Is

A 3-hour, free, hands-on event where attendees replace or dual-boot their Windows/macOS machine with Linux. They arrive scared their computer will break. They leave with a working machine, a physical Roadmap card, and a community to belong to.

The psychological sequence is the product: **Fear → Action → Proof → Community.**

---

## Event Run-of-Show

| Time | Block | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:10 | Welcome + The Why | Founder or host explains TFM mission. Layer 1 (faith) or Layer 2 (privacy) depending on venue. Keep it short — people came to fix computers, not attend a lecture. |
| 0:10–0:25 | Pre-Install Checklist Walkthrough | Walk everyone through the checklist together before anyone touches a machine. Sets expectations, prevents data loss panic. |
| 0:25–2:25 | Installations (Main Block) | Vanguards circulate. Every machine gets hands-on help. No one is left alone with a black screen. |
| 2:25–2:55 | The Send-Off | Every person who finishes gets their physical Roadmap card and a sticker. Group moment. Take a photo. |
| 2:55–3:00 | Next Steps Announcement | Plug the next event (Workshop Series), share the community chat link, pass the donation basket + QR code. |

## What to Bring (Organizer)

- USB drives (3–5 per Vanguard), pre-loaded with: Linux Mint, Pop!_OS, Fedora
- USB-to-Ethernet adapter (1 per Vanguard bag)
- Smartphone with hotspot enabled (backup for venue Wi-Fi)
- Printed Roadmap cards (heavy cardstock)
- Stickers (Linux penguin or TFM logo)
- Donation basket + printed QR code to donation page
- Printed Pre-Install Checklists (one per attendee)
- Printed Terminal Catechism reference cards (give at send-off)

---

## What Attendees Bring

- A laptop they are willing to change
- Power cable (non-negotiable — installation failure from dead battery is the only real risk)
- A backup drive with their important files already copied off
- A list of 3 apps they cannot live without (for FOSS replacement matching)

---

## Pre-Install Checklist (Distribute at Door)

- [ ] Data backed up to external drive or thumb drive
- [ ] "Must-have" apps listed (top 3) — we will find your open source equivalents
- [ ] Machine specs known: touchscreen? Nvidia/AMD GPU? (helps pick the right distro)
- [ ] Power cable in hand

---

## Distro Selection Guide (For Vanguards)

| Machine Type | Recommended Distro |
|---|---|
| Old/slow laptop, first-time user | Linux Mint (Cinnamon) |
| Modern hardware, wants performance | Pop!_OS |
| Privacy-first, intermediate user | Fedora |
| Mac hardware | Fedora or Ubuntu (driver support) |
| Nvidia GPU | Pop!_OS (best out-of-box Nvidia support) |

---

## The Donation Moment

Done at the send-off, not the opening. One line, no pressure:

> *"This event cost you nothing. If it was worth something to you, help us reach the next city."*

Physical basket + QR code. Do not linger on it. Move on.

---

## Event Cadence

- **Year 1 target:** 1 event per month, rotating venues
- **Month 6 milestone:** earliest graduates begin co-hosting
- **Month 12 milestone:** 2 simultaneous events in different locations

---

## Venue Criteria

- Room for 20–50 people with tables (not theater seating — everyone needs a surface)
- Reliable Wi-Fi OR organizer brings hotspot backup
- Accessible parking
- No cost to TFM (this is the ask when canvassing churches — room donation, not rent)

---

## The Psychological Design Note

The Install Party works because it delivers **immediate gratification**. A "dead" or "slow" Windows laptop springing to life on Linux is a testimony. Once they feel the speed and freedom, hunger for Level 2 happens without being pushed. Do not over-program the event. Let the machines do the talking.
