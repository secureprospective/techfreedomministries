## The Governing Principle

The Roadmap is not a curriculum. It is an identity journey. Each level does not just teach something new. It makes someone into someone new.

The four identity shifts, in order:

| Level | The Shift |
|---|---|
| The Exodus | You learn you can leave |
| The Catechism | You learn you belong to something |
| The Homestead | You earn the respect of the room |
| The Great Commission | You become the reason someone else gets out |

Freedom kept to yourself is just a larger cell. That is the thesis of the entire Roadmap and it lives most explicitly in Level 4. But it is present in every level. The Exodus plants the seed. The Great Commission is the harvest.

## The Biblical Framework — Four Levels, Four Anchors

The Roadmap maps onto the arc of Exodus with precision that was not invented — it was discovered.

**The Ten Plagues** — Level 1. The systematic dismantling of every system built to keep people dependent. One by one the infrastructure of control fails. Not all at once. Plague by plague. The operating system goes first. Then the apps. Then the accounts.

**The Parting of the Red Sea** — Level 1. The install moment. The screen goes dark. The room holds its breath. Then it comes back. Faster. Cleaner. Yours. The path through opens and you walk across. There is no going back once you have crossed.

**The Ten Commandments** — Level 1. The Roadmap card. Not a certificate. A covenant. A document that says: here is how a free person maintains their freedom.

**The Wilderness and the Fire** — Level 2. Moses did not teach the wilderness generation in a lecture hall. He taught them around fires, in tents, while they were still dirty from the road. The live sessions are the fire. The archive is the pillar of cloud — always visible, marking the path for anyone who comes later.

**Genesis 1:28** — Level 3. "Be fruitful and increase in number; fill the earth and subdue it." Not conquest. Stewardship. Responsible dominion. The person who pulls their photos off Google and runs them on a server in their own house is exercising biblical stewardship over the digital land they have been given. God did not hand Adam a checklist for subduing the earth. He gave him a garden and let the work define the man.

**Luke 6:40 and Matthew 28:19** — Level 4. A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone when fully trained will be like his teacher. Go and make disciples. Not students. Disciples. People who become like their teacher and then go make more.

## The Felt Experience of Each Level

### The Exodus — What the Room Actually Feels Like

The event opens in controlled chaos. Snacks on the table. Music at low volume. The slideshow running. The founder moving through the room introducing himself. Nobody has started yet but things are clearly in motion. The chaos is the icebreaker. People bond over shared confusion faster than any structured activity.

The cookie in someone's hand is not a detail. First-time attendees are almost always slightly terrified. Give them something to hold and they relax. The check-in person is the first face. That role must be warm, not efficient.

The opening speech is 90 seconds. Everything before it is warmup. It ends with one line:

> *"Freedom isn't a setting you turn on. It's a practice you look after every day."*

That line does three things simultaneously. It is not a tech line. It is not a faith line. It is a human line. Everyone in that room has something in their life they have had to look after. They know what maintenance feels like. The line tells them this is the same thing. It also quietly sets the expectation for the Roadmap without explaining the Roadmap. And it plants the seed that finishing the install is the beginning, not the end.

The 90-minute turn is the most important social moment in the event. The host reads the room and activates the people who finished first. The church callback — "who here has been to church before, go see if your neighbor needs help" — turns the natural human instinct to help into a structured moment. The room becomes its own community in real time, not because anyone told them to bond, but because they were given a reason to.

The send-off is individual. One at a time. With enthusiasm. Every completion is called out to the whole room. The people still working see it happen. They see the card. They want that. Every individual send-off is a live advertisement for finishing.

The event ends when the room decides it does. The last group is still standing around talking and joking and afraid to leave the new friends they just made. That is the goal. Design for the drift.

### The Catechism — The Word and Its Origin

Catechism comes from the Greek *katecheo* — to sound down into. To teach by voice. To echo truth into someone until it resonates on its own. It was the method of the early church before there were buildings, seminaries, or textbooks. A teacher and a student. A question and an answer. Repeated until the student could answer without the teacher present.

The terminal is katecheo made digital. You type a command. The machine responds. You type again. It responds again. You repeat until your fingers know before your brain does. This is the oldest teaching method in human history running on the newest hardware in your house.

The definition is not background context. It is the hook. It belongs on the card early and woven in, not bolted on.

**The community infrastructure at Level 2:**

- Live sessions hosted by the founder — video, real time, open to all members
- Sessions are recorded — the archive becomes the pillar of cloud for anyone who comes later
- Curated library of sourced material so nobody stares at a blank forum
- Community chat channel
- A framework that teaches members to build their own AI advisor — context documents, agent frameworks, a personal technical resource built by the member and owned by the member. Not a corporate product. Not a data-harvesting cloud. This is not giving someone a fish. This is teaching them to build their own boat.

The identity shift at Level 2: at the end of the Exodus, someone knows they can leave. At the end of the Catechism, someone knows they belong to something. The Exodus is escape. The Catechism is formation.

The Great Commission does not start when someone finishes the Roadmap. It starts the first time they answer someone else's question in a live chat. Plant that seed at Level 2 without stating it directly.

### The Homestead — The Most Important Distinction in the Roadmap

The Homestead has no clear finish line. That is not a weakness. It is the most important distinction in the entire Roadmap.

Levels 1 and 2 have clear finish lines. Linux boots. The script runs. You can point to the moment it happened. Level 3 does not work that way. Nobody wakes up and says "I am now a Homesteader." Someone else looks at what they built and says — that person did real work.

The Homestead is identified by peers, not by a checklist. It is proof of work, not proof of attendance. Not proof of enrollment.

A Homestead can be built on a $35 single-board computer or an old desktop pulled from a closet. The work is what makes it a homestead. Not the budget. This must appear on the card before anyone talks themselves out of starting.

What a Homesteader might be running: a home server, a self-hosted media library, a full network build with their own DNS filtering and intrusion detection, a home theater on open source hardware. The specific thing varies. That is the point.

The Homesteader does not just have a system. They can explain it. They can defend it. They can teach it. That is what separates Level 3 from Level 2. Level 2 teaches you the language. Level 3 is what you build once you can speak it.

The most visceral image on this card: the midnight realization that your local DNS filter just blocked ten thousand corporate telemetry pings, and it did it because you built it that way.

### The Great Commission — Discipleship as Strategy

One word defines Level 4: discipleship.

You are only as free as your friends and family. You can easily be imprisoned by your neighbor. The person next to you at church, at work, at the dinner table — if they are still tracked and sold, your proximity to that system never fully ends. This is not sentiment. This is a strategic reality.

Luke 6:40. A disciple is not above his teacher, but everyone when fully trained will be like his teacher. The Vanguard is not a superior. They are a mirror of what the mission produces. And the mission is only complete when the mirror turns around and shows someone else what they could become.

The Level 4 graduate is not defined by technical mastery. They are defined by the decision to return. To go back to the room where they once sat scared and be the reason someone else does not have to be. The willingness to return is the only qualification that matters.

The Vanguard's Final Blessing — said at events, with a straight face, and it lands every time:

> *"May your dependencies be satisfied, your kernel be stable, and your uptime be long. Go in peace to serve your machine, so it may serve you."*

This blessing belongs to the event ceremony. It does not appear on the card.

## Words That Do Not Appear in TFM Copy

**Plantation** — carries negative social impact that distracts from the mission. The cage metaphor carries the same weight without the baggage. This word does not appear in any TFM copy, in any layer, in any context.

**Stakeholders, deliverables, ecosystem, leverage** — corporate nonprofit language. Never.

**Completion, graduation, certification** at Level 3 — the Homestead has no finish line. Language that implies one undermines the peer recognition dynamic.

## The Gemini Workflow — How These Cards Were Built

Each card followed the same process:

1. Conversation with the founder to establish the felt experience of the level
2. A structured Gemini prompt built on real ground — not elevator pitch assumptions
3. Gemini output reviewed and pressure-tested against TFM voice and theology
4. Best angles identified and combined
5. Founder provides the send-off line — spoken off the top of their head when asked the right question
6. Card built in the design system and previewed
7. HTML handed to Claude Code for site implementation

The Gemini prompt is always built on what the founder knows, not what an outsider guesses. Gemini's output is treated as a well-informed first draft from someone who got the elevator pitch. The real work happens before the prompt is written and after the output comes back.

## Copy Rules Specific to the Roadmap Cards

- The hook lives in the dark leather header. It is the first thing someone reads when the card opens.
- The italic gold subhead in the header is the bridge — it connects the hook to what follows.
- The three movements are the spine of every card. They carry the biblical framework without explaining it.
- The stamp frame closes every card. It holds the scripture reference and the founder's send-off line. The scripture is in italic Georgia. The send-off is in gold. They are never swapped.
- The ghost button at the bottom of every card drives to the next level. The ghost button on Level 4 drives back to Level 1. The Roadmap is a loop, not a line.
- The What It Costs / What It Changes grid is present on every card. It is the honest accounting of the level. It does not sugarcoat.
- The five milestones use the names from TFM_03. They do not get renamed.

## The Narrative Arc Across All Four Cards

A visitor who reads all four cards in sequence experiences a complete story:

They arrive scared something is wrong. They are told they were right. They are given a machine and a community and a set of skills and a property. And then they are told that none of it is complete until they go back for someone else.

The Exodus gives them a door. The Catechism gives them a language. The Homestead gives them a house. The Great Commission gives them a reason to leave the house and hold the door open.

That is the Roadmap. That is TFM. That is the fishing pole.
